# Animation principles applied to video

The classic principles still hold. Here is how each one works in promo and social video.

| Principle | In video work | Quick test |
|---|---|---|
| **Timing** | Duration sets weight and mood | Could the same move be 30% shorter without loss? |
| **Easing** | Acceleration shapes personality | Does the curve match the tone? |
| **Anticipation** | A tiny pre-move before the main move (a 3–6 frame pull-back, a flash before a reveal) | Does the big move feel like it came from nowhere? |
| **Follow-through and overlap** | Not everything stops at once. Tail words, shadows, and secondary elements settle slightly later | Do all elements land on the same frame? Stagger them |
| **Staging** | One clear focus per moment. Dim or remove the rest | Can you name the one thing the eye should see? |
| **Arcs** | Natural moves curve slightly. Use a small curved path or rotate while moving | Do straight diagonal slides look stiff? |
| **Secondary action** | A small supporting motion that adds life (a glow pulse, a particle drift) | Is the frame ever fully still? |
| **Exaggeration** | Push scale and speed beyond reality for clarity | Is the key word big enough to read in half a second? |
| **Squash and stretch** | Use sparingly, mostly for playful tones | Never on serious or premium tones |

## Choreography: several things at once

1. **Lead with one hero move.** Everything else supports it.
2. **Stagger, do not sync.** Offset related items by 2–6 frames (0.04–0.1 s for small items, 0.3–0.4 s for words in a sentence).
3. **Same direction in a group.** If four cards enter, they enter from the same side.
4. **Match velocity at seams.** If an element exits fast, the next one should enter at a matching speed. See `transitions-and-camera/seam-design.md`.
5. **Leave breathing room.** After about 7–8 s of dense motion, give 1 s of rest.

## Hierarchy of motion

Rank what moves by importance and give the biggest, fastest motion to the most important thing. If everything moves at the same intensity, nothing stands out.

1. The message (key word, key number)
2. The product or subject
3. Supporting labels
4. Background texture

## Keep motion motivated

Every move needs a reason: it reveals something, directs attention, shows cause and effect, or carries energy into a cut. Motion for its own sake reads as filler.
