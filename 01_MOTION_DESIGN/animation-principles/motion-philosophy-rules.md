# Motion philosophy: the house laws

Distilled from a 30-second reference spot analysed in the Herk kit, then merged with the variety rules of the saas-motion-kit. These are a **style**, tuned for dark, premium, tech-brand films. Pick the laws that fit your tone. Do not apply all of them to a warm or playful piece.

## Core laws

1. **One idea per beat.** Average scene about 1.5 s in a dense film. If a scene says two things, split it.
2. **Negative space is design.** In the dark premium style, about 90% of the frame is near-black.
3. **Light carries the brand.** Gradients, halos, vignettes, beams. The piece is lit rather than coloured.
4. **The camera never sleeps.** Slow drifts, rotating objects, breathing vignettes. A fully static frame reads as dead.
5. **Motion blur is a feature.** Ride every transition on a blur or streak. It hides the cut and adds energy.
6. **Objects carry meaning.** Give each recurring object one job (broken, working, fast). Bring the same object back at least once.
7. **Colour is symbolic.** Each colour owns one idea. If you cannot say what a colour means, remove it. Cap at five active hues.
8. **Type is a character.** Words scale, morph, glow. In a type-led film, text drives most of the story.
9. **Hold the hero shot.** Let the logo or key reveal sit for 1.5–2 s. Let the end card breathe.
10. **One unifying texture.** A grid, a grain, a recurring shape. It glues the scenes together.
11. **Callbacks beat novelty.** One visual that returns later feels designed.

## Three-part structure

Think in threes: problem → brand; benefits → surfaces → products; foundation → call to action → silence. Three benefits, three surfaces, three names.

## Pacing numbers (dense style)

- Scene length: 1.0–2.0 s. Longer only for hero moments and the outro.
- A new element every 0.3–0.6 s inside a scene.
- No dead air over 1 s in the middle of the piece, except deliberate holds.
- Word stagger: 0.3–0.4 s for narrative lines, 0.5–0.6 s for dramatic ones.
- Whip transition: 0.3–0.4 s.
- Logo hold 1.5–2 s. CTA card 4–6 s. Section headlines 1–1.5 s after fully revealed.

## Where these laws do not apply

Calm, trustworthy, warm, or nostalgic tones want longer holds and fewer cuts. Use `tone-matrix.md` to override the pacing numbers above. A rule becomes a mistake when it fights the tone.

## Pre-flight (motion films)

- [ ] Average scene length fits the tone
- [ ] No dead air longer than 1 s outside deliberate holds
- [ ] Every transition uses motion, not a default fade, unless the tone calls for it
- [ ] Palette within five hues, each with a meaning
- [ ] At least one callback
- [ ] Hero shot and end card held long enough
- [ ] Frames actually viewed (a lint pass is not a design review)
- [ ] All tween end times land on frame boundaries
