# Sound effect palette rules

## Principle

**Sound effects follow visible actions.** They do not decorate every cut. If the viewer cannot see why the sound happens, drop it.

## Palette by tone

| Tone | Palette |
|---|---|
| Calm / warm | Soft taps, paper, wood, air, gentle room reverb |
| Trustworthy | Warm marimba or wood block in one key, one soft confirm tone |
| Technical | Dry clicks, keyboard, data blips |
| Playful | Pops, plucks, 8-bit bleeps |
| Premium | Low drones, a single piano note, silence |
| Urgent | Ticks, rising pitch, hard stops |
| Bold | Kick-like hits, stabs |

## Map action → sound

| Visible action | Sound |
|---|---|
| Real spatial move (slide, swipe) | Soft air whoosh |
| Logo or object lands | Damped tap or low thud |
| Small UI interaction | Dry click |
| Document or card | Paper sound |
| Success / check | One short warm tone |
| Error / X | A dry stop, not a buzzer |
| Big reveal | Swell into a hit, or a deliberate drop to silence |

## Avoid

- Laser zaps, pitch-drop plops, sci-fi chirps.
- A whoosh on every cut.
- Pure sine beeps, which sound thin and eerie. Warm tuned timbres (marimba, wood block) in one key feel friendlier.
- Reverb tails so long they bleed into the next beat unintentionally.
- The same sample repeated on a list. Rotate between several recordings of the same sound (click, tap, ceramic touch), or the list will sound like a percussion loop.

## Level

Effects sit well below voice (a typical range is 15–20 dB below the voice peak, depending on type). Tails may bleed into the next beat; that is natural. Silence can be the loudest effect: a deliberate cut to true silence on a drop is memorable.

## Sync

- Sound leads picture by a few frames for impact.
- Put effects on the same beat grid as the picture if the timeline can move.
- After any retime, check every effect against its action.

## Licensing

Keep a licence note for every sample or library. Commercial work needs licensed or original sounds. Synthesised effects you generate yourself have no licence risk.
