# Music selection and music bed

## Pick by mood and job

| Content | Music direction |
|---|---|
| Claims and news | Darker, confident, steady beat |
| Methods and how-tos | Lo-fi, chill groove |
| Premium brand | Sparse, low, cinematic; allow silence |
| Playful product | Plucky, syncopated, bright |
| Calm explainer | Airy pads, soft texture |
| Celebration | Build, then release |

## Level

- The bed sits about **11 dB under the voice** (working range about 9–14 dB).
- Duck the bed under speech and let it rise in gaps.
- In short-form, the first 1–2 seconds are often quieter, with the beat landing on the second line.
- With no voice (pure motion film), a "warm pad" bed at a very low level can work better than a full music track.

## Structure

- Fit the music to the cut, not the cut to the music, unless the piece is music-led.
- In a music-led film, build the **beat grid** first, then cut to it. Measure the beat grid of the actual track and use that.
- Land important moments on strong beats or bar starts.
- Plan the ending: let the music resolve or cut to silence on purpose. Never fade out just because the picture ended.

## Practical

- Keep stems (drums, bass, melody) when available. You can drop the drums under a spoken key line.
- Avoid music with prominent vocals under speech.
- For loops (a seamless film loop), use a silent film or a track that loops cleanly.

## Where to get music and SFX (verify-first)

| Source | Good for | Watch out |
|---|---|---|
| YouTube Audio Library | YouTube videos | Some tracks require attribution; terms are for YouTube use |
| Pixabay Music / SFX | Free commercial use under its licence | Read the current licence; content ID claims happen on popular tracks |
| Freesound | SFX | Licence is per file (CC0, CC BY, CC BY-NC). NC files are not for paid work |
| Paid subscriptions (Epidemic Sound, Artlist, Musicbed, Uppbeat Premium) | Client work, ads | Licence covers the subscriber's channels or projects; check client and paid-ad coverage |
| Engine tools (HyperFrames `media-use` SFX set and music generation) | Drafts, own content | Check each model's licence; many music models are non-commercial |
| Your own synthesised SFX | Anything | No licence risk |

## Licensing

- Know the licence before you edit. Commercial and platform use differ from personal use.
- Many open-weight music generation models carry non-commercial terms. Do not use their output in paid client work without checking the licence.
- Keep a record: track name, source, licence, and where it is used.
- Platform audio libraries often work only on that platform. Check before reusing the same cut elsewhere.
