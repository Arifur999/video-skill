# Reference credit policy

Learn from real videos in the open. Credit every one. Borrow grammar, never assets.

## The rules

1. **Credit every reference** by creator name and link. No anonymous "inspired by".
2. **Borrow grammar only:** rhythm, structure, transition types, camera ideas, pacing patterns.
3. **Never borrow assets:** footage, music, characters, logos, copy, or distinctive visual identity.
4. **Keep other people's work out of the repo.** Store reference clips and breakdown outputs in an ignored folder (for example `.references/`).
5. **Mark borrowed shots** in the storyboard ledger with `ref:<id>`, and list each reference with creator and link next to the storyboard. A `ref:` with no credited row is an error.
6. **Ask when unsure.** If a reference is very distinctive, change more than you borrow.

## Breakdown outputs

A shot-by-shot breakdown of a reference may include: shot count, cuts per 10 s, shot lengths, how fast the first 3 s go, a contact sheet of one frame per shot, and a draft ledger. Every breakdown opens with a credit header (creator, title, link, date analysed).

## Why this matters for an agency

Clients can be sued for copied work, and so can you. A clear credit trail protects you, helps clients trust you, and keeps your own style improving rather than drifting into copying.
