# Palette and colour meaning

## The rule

**Each colour in a film owns one idea.** If you cannot say what a colour means, remove it. Cap the film at about five active hues, each with a job.

## Example mapping (adapt to the brand)

| Colour role | Meaning in the story |
|---|---|
| Red | Problem, loss, wrong way |
| Teal / green | Solution, working, payoff |
| Blue | Connection, trust, global |
| Purple / magenta | Speed, energy, API-like |
| Amber / yellow | Value, affordability, warning-light attention |
| Neutral chrome / white | Brand voice, premium |

These are conventions, not laws. A finance brand may use green for risk. Choose, write it down, and stay consistent.

## Use of accent

- No more than two accent colours in one shot.
- Colour the whole chunk or object, not a random letter.
- In captions, colour 25–45% of chunks at most (see `captions-and-titles/caption-style-rules.md`).

## Brand colour handling

- Take colours from the brand's official tokens.
- Check contrast against backgrounds (aim for strong contrast for text; a ratio of at least 4.5:1 is the usual accessibility baseline for body text).
- Test brand colours on a phone in daylight and in a dark room.

## Colour as story device

- **Recolour without cutting:** change the colour variables so the same layout shifts meaning (for example, from "global" blue to "affordable" amber).
- **Colour flood** as a surprise or act break.
- **Return the same colour** for the same idea in a callback.

## Dark-ground palettes

Near-black grounds make colours feel luminous. Give colour a tiny bit of lift so it does not vanish on compressed video, and avoid pure saturated blue on black for text, which has poor legibility.
