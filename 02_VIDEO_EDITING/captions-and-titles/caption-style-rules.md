# Caption style rules

For burned-in, word-driven captions on short-form video. For standard subtitles, see `subtitle-timing-standards.md`.

## Chunking and timing

- **1–3 words per chunk**, split on phrase boundaries, never mid-phrase.
- **Hard swap** between chunks. No pop or bounce animation by default.
- Show a chunk about **2 frames before** its first spoken word, so the eye is ready.
- Time every chunk from word-level timestamps, not from a rough script.

## Look

- **One look for the whole video.** Same box, same font, same size, same edge. Mixing boxed and bare captions looks cheap.
- A heavy sans in all caps at about 62–64 px (on a 1080-wide frame) reads well. A heavy serif accent on about 5–20% of chunks, for soft words, adds variety. Never two serif chunks in a row.
- **Colour on 25–45% of chunks, whole chunk only.** Example roles: red for a wrong way or a loss, teal for the concept or keyword, green for the action or payoff.
- **Dark box with a thin light edge.** The box gives contrast on bright footage; the edge keeps it visible on a dark background.

## Contrast: measure, do not guess

**Never white on light.** Sample the pixels behind every caption and check them. If a shot is bright, the dark box does the work. If a shot is dark, the thin light edge does it.

## Position

- Place on the seam in a split layout.
- Place on the chest area in a punch-in, **not** the jaw.
- **Never over the mouth or chin**, least of all in the first second, when the viewer decides whether the person on screen is real.
- When a caption runs across a cut, move it to the new shot's position on the cut frame.
- On shots full of text to read, move the caption to a clear zone instead of hiding it. **Captions should not blink out.**
- Stay inside platform safe zones (top and bottom bands are covered by app UI).

## Accuracy

- Every word must match what is said.
- Check names, brands, and numbers by eye.
- Do not caption music lyrics unless needed.

## Logos and tool names

When the speaker names a tool and points to it, a quiet logo pop at that spot can replace a caption. Use the real logo, with permission where needed.

## Accessibility

- Burned-in captions are not a substitute for a separate caption file when a platform supports one. Provide an SRT/VTT for long-form.
- Do not rely on colour alone to carry meaning.
