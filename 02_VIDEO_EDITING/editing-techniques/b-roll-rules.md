# B-roll rules

**Video beats still images, every time.** Every shot should move.

## Preference order

1. **Real footage of the thing named:** the person's talk, a product demo, your own earlier video.
2. **Real screen recording** of the site or app, zoomed into the part being discussed, with a visible cursor.
3. **Animated UI mock:** typing, rows ticking, a counter climbing. Label it as an example.
4. **AI-generated video.** Concrete metaphors work (a bill burning for "wasting money"; a slot machine for "hoping it works"). Generic "person at a computer" looks like stock.
5. **Short highlight crops of a real page** (under about 1 s) on tool names, prices, badges.

AI-generated *stills* as B-roll are the weakest choice. Use them only as the first frame of an AI video.

## Rules that came from mistakes

- **Never invent numbers, results, or tests** to decorate a claim.
- **Crop, then highlight.** A whole web page shrunk to phone size is unreadable. Crop to the phrase and highlight it on the spoken word.
- **Fill the panel.** Small UI floating in empty space is the fastest "AI edit" tell.
- **Do not click Accept** on cookie or terms dialogs while recording.
- **Check continuity of generated people:** same outfit, same room across shots; pass the previous frame as a reference.
- **AI video quirks:** many models switch to a second camera angle around 2.5–3 s, and on-screen text turns to gibberish. Use the first continuous seconds and keep screens small in frame.
- **Use each B-roll scene once** per piece.
- **Show every item in a spoken list** with its own shot.
- **Stay truthful.** Generated people are actors, not customers or testimonials.

## Anchoring

Tie each B-roll shot to the exact spoken word or noun it illustrates. If it appears early or late, it confuses rather than helps.

## Layout patterns for vertical video (1080×1920)

- **Split:** B-roll on top, speaker below.
- **Full B-roll:** footage fills the frame.
- **Punch-in:** tight shot of the speaker for emphasis, pivots, and the call to action.

Keep the speaker on screen a meaningful share of the time and never gone for more than a few seconds.
